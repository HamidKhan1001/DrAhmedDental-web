Mandibular left central incisor

The smallest tooth of the permanent dentition: a narrow, symmetrical incisor with a single root that is flattened mesiodistally.

Form and anatomy

The crown is narrow mesiodistally and almost perfectly symmetrical, with sharp, nearly right-angled incisal corners and a straight incisal edge that lies slightly lingual to the long axis of the root. The labial surface is flat to slightly convex; the lingual surface is smooth, with a shallow lingual fossa and faint marginal ridges above a small cingulum. Right and left central incisors are very hard to tell apart. The root is long and ribbon-like: narrow mesiodistally but wide labiolingually, often with shallow depressions on its mesial and distal surfaces. These depressions show why a single root may still carry two canals, one labial and one lingual, which usually rejoin near the apex. The thin alveolar bone over the labial surface is easily lost in periodontal disease.

Function

Cutting food against the maxillary incisors.

Clinical relevance

Although usually single-rooted, a second (lingual) canal is present in a notable minority.
